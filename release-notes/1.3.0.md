title: Today's plan keeps up with your day
---

Your plan for the day now follows you from clock-in to clock-out: it becomes a
checklist you work through, Huddle hears about each ticket you finish, and
clocking out carries whatever you didn't get to over to your next session.

## Your plan becomes a checklist

When you clock in with a plan, the editor you wrote it in turns it into a
checklist: each ticket gets its own line with a timer button in front of it, and
the one you're working on shows its time under the button. Your wrap-up starts
from that same checklist, with your checks in it. Your plan post in Huddle
stays exactly as your team read it; what happens after it shows up as new posts
below it.

- **Touch and go.** Switching from one ticket to another checks the first one
  off, and Huddle says so: "Switched to TH-2 · TH-1 checked off at 1h 10m".
  Go back to a ticket and it's unchecked again, with its timer picking up
  where it left off.
- **Check items off by hand**, or uncheck them, right in the plan. Each one is
  posted to Huddle.
- **Start from the plan.** Each ticket in your plan has a timer button in front
  of it on the Clock page and in your plan post in Huddle. Start a ticket you've
  since taken out of the plan, and TimeHuddle asks whether to add it back.
- **The running timer, where you press it.** On a ticket's page and in your
  plan, the running ticket's time shows right under its timer button; on My
  Board it's the green badge beside the title. In Huddle, your plan post has
  just the buttons, and the "Started …" and "Switched to …" updates are plain
  text.
- **My Board** shows each plan ticket's time this session, and the running
  ticket's as it ticks.

## Clocking out carries your plan over

When you clock out, the tickets you checked off in today's plan leave Pinned
and go back to the rest of your board. The ones you didn't get to stay pinned,
in your order, ready for your next session. If a ticket's timer is still
running, TimeHuddle asks first: check it off, or keep it pinned for next time.

Your wrap-up updates the plan you posted that morning, so the day reads as one
post in Huddle: today's checklist shows what you checked off, with your wrap-up
under it. A wrap-up recorded with Pulse is posted as its own video.

## Continue Working keeps you clocked in

Choosing **Continue Working** on the shift-end reminder now keeps you clocked in
past 8 hours. Before, it only closed the reminder, and you were clocked out at
8 hours anyway.

When you are clocked out automatically, because you agreed to it or didn't
answer the reminder, you now get a notification saying so, under the bell and
as a pop-up. It follows your **Shift-end reminder** notification settings, and
tapping it opens that session in Huddle. Your team's admins are told too, the same way they are
when you clock out yourself.

## Automatic clock-outs show on your profile

When TimeHuddle clocks you out at 8 hours, the Activity tab on your profile now
shows it as **Auto clocked out**, with the team and how long the shift was.
Before, it showed only that you clocked in.

## Tickets on your phone

In the TimeHuddle app, tapping the search bar on Tickets opens a full-screen
search. Results are laid out like your ticket rows and scroll freely, and the
keyboard goes away as you scroll. **Cancel** closes it.

On a phone, New ticket and the search bar stay at the top as you scroll your
tickets. The tabs and the filter bar fold away to give the list more room, and
come back when you scroll to the top.

## Which build you're running

A new **About** page shows which build of the app you're running and which
server it talks to: the version, when each was built or started, the server's
address and the commit. A test build also shows its branch and the changes
it has that aren't released yet. Open it from **About** in the account menu,
or tap the version number at the bottom of Settings or of the More menu on a
phone. If you report a problem, include what it says.
