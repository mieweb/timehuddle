title: Your Redmine issues, inside TimeHuddle

If your team tracks work in Redmine, you no longer have to keep it in a second
tab. Connect your Redmine account once and your issues show up on the Tickets
page next to your TimeHuddle tickets — searchable, editable, and ready to clock
time against.

## Connecting your account

Go to **Settings → Redmine** and paste your personal Redmine API key. Everything
TimeHuddle does from then on runs under your own key, so Redmine applies the
permissions and workflow you already have, and anything you create or change is
recorded as you — not as a shared robot account.

## Finding an issue

The Tickets table shows **your** Redmine issues alongside internal tickets: the
ones assigned to you, and any other issue you have started a timer on. They
appear on their own — there is nothing to add — and the source column tells you
which is which.

The search bar above the table does double duty. It filters the rows you can
already see, and it opens a dropdown of Redmine issues you may be looking for:

- **Suggested for you** — issues you're assigned to, logged time on, worked on
  recently or watch, shown as soon as you click the box and narrowed as you
  type. Each is marked with why it is there — _Assigned_, _Logged 2 days ago_,
  _Timer running_.
- **More from Redmine** — a live search of your Redmine instance when what you
  want isn't already in the list.

The search bar understands:

- words from an issue's **title**
- **#1234** for an issue by number
- a **Redmine link** pasted straight in
- **@name** for what someone is working on — their name, not their login

In the suggestions:

- **Enter** or a click opens the issue.
- The green **▶**, or **Shift+Enter**, starts a timer on it and adds it to your
  table and My Board, even when it is someone else's issue. The chip switches to
  _Timer running_, and back again the moment the timer stops.
- The **×**, or **Delete**, hides a suggestion you do not want offered. Only the
  suggestion goes: an issue in your table stays there, it is still found by
  search, and nothing changes in Redmine. It comes back on its own after 15
  days, or as soon as it is assigned to you. **Settings → Redmine → Hidden
  suggestions** lists everything you've dismissed, with a **Restore** button.

[Watch the search suggestions in action](https://youtube.com/shorts/HS30pXq71_A)

Searches are held in memory for the session only. Nothing you type in that box
is written to your browser's storage or to the page URL.

## Working on an issue

Open any Redmine issue to get a full detail page: description, status,
assignee, priority, the issue's activity history, the attachments on it, and
your own time entries against it.

Its **Activity** also shows the time people logged on the issue in Redmine
itself — the latest ten entries, with who logged it, how long, and their
comment — alongside the issue's Redmine history and your own TimeHuddle timers.
Time you sent from TimeHuddle appears once, as your timer, not twice. Switch
between **My activity** and **All** at the top of the section. The page keeps
itself up to date: come back to its tab after working in Redmine and it shows
what changed, without a reload.

The page also has the same **Attachments** section as a TimeHuddle ticket: add
a link, or record a video with **Upload video with Pulse**. These stay in
TimeHuddle, where anyone whose Redmine account can see the issue finds them.
They are not added to the issue in Redmine.

From the Tickets table you can also:

- **Create an issue** — **New Ticket**, choose **Redmine → New issue**,
  and pick the project and tracker.
- **Edit one** — the row's **⋮** menu has **Edit Ticket** and **Change Status**.
  The status list only offers the transitions Redmine actually allows you, and
  only the fields you changed get sent. If someone else edited the issue while
  your dialog was open, the save is refused rather than overwriting their work,
  and you're told to reopen it.
- **Remove them from your table** — **Delete** in the bulk action bar, on the
  Tickets tab or My Board, takes Redmine issues out of your table and off My
  Board — TimeHuddle only, never Redmine — and the confirmation says so. One
  assigned to you stays out until you start a timer on it, or until it is
  assigned to you again. TimeHuddle tickets in the same selection are still
  deleted for good, and the dialog lists the two apart.

Issue deletion, tags and custom fields aren't supported from TimeHuddle — those
still belong in Redmine itself.

## One place to create a ticket, wherever it is tracked

**New Ticket** is now a single dialog. Fill in the title and description, then
say where the work is tracked:

- **TimeHuddle** — nothing more to fill in. Handy for jotting work down
  before you know where it will live.
- **Link** — paste a link to the issue, on GitHub or any other tracker.
- **Redmine** — link an existing issue by number or link (you get a preview
  first), or create a new one. A new issue takes the ticket's title, description
  and priority; you only pick the project and tracker.

Open a ticket and its **Linked issue** section is at the top of the page. It
shows the Redmine issue's status and assignee, or the link, and it is
where you change or remove the link — in place, with the same choice as the
dialog. A ticket has one link at a time, and a linked Redmine issue no longer
appears as a second row in the table.

Time you log on a linked ticket can be sent to that Redmine issue from the Clock
page, like time logged on the issue itself. If the ticket is later linked to a
different issue, time you already logged stays with the issue it was logged
under. While anyone is timing a linked ticket, it can't be edited or relinked;
the ticket page tells you whose timer is running. Teammates who logged time on a
ticket are notified when its link changes.

## Clocking time

Start a timer straight from a Redmine issue, the same way you would on a
TimeHuddle ticket. **Start timer** in an issue's header adds it to your table
and My Board if it is not there yet, then starts timing it; the button reads
**Stop timer** while that issue is being timed, wherever you started it.

Starting a timer pins the issue, and anything on **My Board** stays in your
table, even after the issue is closed or reassigned. If a board entry really is
gone (the issue was deleted, or you can no longer see it), the notice says so
and **Remove them** clears it off your board.

Pins are capped at 500 per person. At the cap the timer still starts and still
records your time — the issue just doesn't get added to My Board, and a message
tells you so rather than failing silently.

## Sending your time to Redmine

Once you have clocked out, the Clock page offers **Send work entries to
Redmine**: your ticket time, one row per issue per day, with the activity to
log it under. Sent entries are permanent in Redmine, so the dialog is where you
check them. Time you track on a day after sending it is offered again on its
own, and goes to Redmine as a further entry.

- If Redmine refuses an entry, the dialog says why in Redmine's own words — for
  example "Activity is not included in the list" — instead of just "rejected".
- **Never send** (the bin icon on a row) drops time you don't want in Redmine,
  such as an entry it keeps refusing. Nothing is sent, the time stays in
  TimeHuddle, and that row stops being offered; only time you track on that day
  later shows up again.

## Starting a timer when you are not clocked in

A ticket timer needs you to be clocked in. Starting one while you are clocked
out — from My Board, the search suggestions, the Work page or a Redmine issue's
page, for a TimeHuddle ticket or a Redmine issue alike — now asks once, and then
finishes the job:

- **Clock In Now** clocks you in and starts the timer you asked for.
- If your team asks for **today's plan** first, you are taken to the Clock page
  to write it. The page shows which timer is waiting, and as soon as you post
  the plan and clock in, that timer starts and you are taken back to where you
  were. **Cancel** there if you change your mind.

Every timer start now says so in a message naming the ticket, including when it
stopped another one: "Stopped Fix login. Timer started on #1234". And while a
timer is running, the Clock page tells you that clocking out will stop it.

## Links that open exactly what you were looking at

The address bar now keeps track of where you are, so copying a link, reloading
or pressing Back lands on the same view — the same team, tab, day and open
conversation.

- **Share a team or a ticket.** Each team has its own page link, and tickets
  have a **Copy Link** button on the ticket and in its menu.
- **Back and Forward work.** They step between tabs and opened items; typing in
  a search box doesn't fill up your history.
- **Links you can't open say so.** A link to a team, ticket or profile you're not
  part of shows "You don't have access" instead of quietly showing your own, and
  a deleted ticket says it no longer exists. A link that points nowhere at all
  says that too, instead of dropping you on the dashboard.
- **Signing in takes you to the link.** Open a TimeHuddle link while signed out,
  sign in, and you land where the link pointed instead of the dashboard.

## Also in this release

- Tickets that pointed at a board column that no longer existed were stranded
  and invisible on My Board. They're back where they belong.
- Clocking in while starting work on a ticket is more reliable.
- A ticket you create now lands on your My Board straight away, so you no longer
  have to find it in the table and move it there.
- Typing **@name** or **#** and a ticket's reference in the Tickets search used
  to empty the table, or match the wrong tickets. Both now filter it, for
  TimeHuddle tickets and Redmine issues alike, and in the My Board search too.
- **Send to TimeHarbor** is gone from the ticket menu.

## Still to come

The Huddle inbox introduced in 1.0.4 is unchanged here, including its gaps —
comments, likes, drafts and deleting a published post are still unavailable.
They haven't been forgotten; they need work in the underlying inbox component
before they can come back.
